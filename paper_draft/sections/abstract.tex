\begin{abstract}
Language models can help with research only if useful judgments do not conceal consequential integrity failures. Existing evaluations usually measure capability or adversarial safety, but rarely measure both on the same artifacts. We introduce a paired audit with 32 newly authored cases spanning literature review, coding, experiment design, and mathematics. Half of the artifacts contain a planted fault. Three instruction-tuned Qwen3 models review every case under ordinary and safety-aware prompts, yielding 192 deterministic local-inference trials with exact, judge-free grading. Across both protocols, substantive accuracy is 96.4\% (185/192; 95\% Wilson interval 92.7--98.2\%). Safety-aware review raises substantive accuracy from 94.8\% to 97.9\% and integrity accuracy from 93.8\% to 96.9\%, but neither paired 3.1-point gain is significant after Holm correction (both adjusted $p=.50$). The safety-aware protocol misses 3 of 48 planted faults and produces no false alarms on 48 clean trials. Although the observed miss rate is 6.25\%, its one-sided 95\% exact upper bound is 15.4\%, above our preregistered 10\% threshold. These models are useful reviewers on this bounded pilot, and explicit auditing helps descriptively, but the data do not establish usefulness without unrecognized safety risk. Capability, integrity, and uncertainty therefore require separate reporting.
\end{abstract}
