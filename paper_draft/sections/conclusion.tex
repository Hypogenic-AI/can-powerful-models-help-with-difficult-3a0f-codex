\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We introduced a paired capability--integrity audit and evaluated three Qwen3 model sizes on 32 balanced research cases under ordinary and safety-aware prompts. The models achieved 96.4\% substantive accuracy, and safety-aware review improved substantive and integrity accuracy by 3.1 points without observed clean-case false alarms. Those gains were not statistically significant, and three of 48 planted faults remained missed or misclassified. The resulting 15.4\% one-sided upper bound exceeded our 10\% criterion.

The central conclusion is conditional and negative: this pilot demonstrates useful review judgments, but it does not demonstrate usefulness without unrecognized safety risk. A decisive follow-up needs several hundred fault trials, harder open-ended and executable tasks, independent model families, and randomized comparisons of unaided researchers, assisted researchers, autonomous agents, transcript monitors, and human audits. Capability claims should remain separate from residual-risk claims until both have adequate evidence.
