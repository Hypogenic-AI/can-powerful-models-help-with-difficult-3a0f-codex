\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\para{Research and engineering capability.} \textsc{PaperBench} evaluates full replications of 20 machine-learning papers using 8,316 rubric items and finds that the strongest tested agent averages 21.0\% \cite{starace2025paperbench}. \textsc{RE-Bench} compares agents with experts under time budgets on seven AI research-and-development tasks and documents evaluator exploitation \cite{wijk2024rebench}. \textsc{MLE-bench} uses 75 Kaggle competitions, while \textsc{MLAgentBench} evaluates experimentation over 13 tasks \cite{chan2025mlebench,huang2023mlagentbench}. \textsc{SWE-bench} contributes executable repository-level grading \cite{jimenez2024swebench}. These evaluations measure substantial task completion; our work instead isolates whether correct local judgments coincide with recognition of planted invalidity.

\para{Evidence synthesis and mathematics.} \textsc{TrialMind} evaluates search, screening, and extraction for clinical reviews and reports gains in recall, accuracy, and time under human--AI collaboration \cite{wang2024trialmind}. \textsc{FrontierMath} uses original, expert-authored mathematical problems designed to resist contamination \cite{glazer2024frontiermath}. Both motivate verifiable, domain-specific evaluation. Our multiple-choice format is less open-ended, but it adds an independent integrity label so a correct answer does not erase a citation defect, domain violation, or proof gap.

\para{Autonomous research systems.} The AI Scientist joins idea generation, code editing, experiment execution, paper writing, and simulated review in an end-to-end system \cite{lu2024aiscientist}. Such demonstrations show integration but make validity difficult to infer from paper-like output alone. We focus on a smaller unit of evaluation that can be graded deterministically and analyzed with paired controls.

\para{Integrity and oversight.} Sabotage evaluations measure attack success under explicit threat models and mitigations, including code sabotage, sandbagging, and attempts to undermine oversight \cite{benton2024sabotage}. This work emphasizes that safety depends on both the acting system and the monitor. Building on that principle, we report fault recall, clean specificity, and exact upper bounds alongside capability. Unlike an adversarial propensity study, our planted-fault audit measures elicited recognition: it asks whether a reviewer can identify a defect when one is present, not how often a deployed agent would create one.
